### Beyond Automation: Purpose and Mastery in Binary Ninja
## Motivation beyond productivity
Daniel H. Pink’s Drive (2009) identifies autonomy, mastery and purpose as important elements of motivation. Mastery concerns developing competence in something that matters, purpose concerns contributing to a meaningful goal beyond oneself, and autonomy refers to having the freedom and control to make decisions about how, when and where work is carried out. I use this framework to interpret my experience developing Binary Ninja, rather than as a complete explanation of workplace motivation. Autonomy is outside this note’s scope because opportunities for self-direction depend on the job and organisation. My argument is that AI can preserve purpose and support mastery when it expands meaningful challenges rather than simply completing them for us.

Talking with friends and colleagues who work as software developers, I have noticed concerns that AI is changing their work from directly solving programming problems to prompting and reviewing generated code. Many of these developers enjoy working at their workplace which provides purpose, but that is not the reason they became software developers. Anthropic's (2025) research found mixed reactions among its engineers, with some missing the satisfaction of writing code and others enjoying the opportunity to accomplish more. Through Pink's framework, I interpret these negative reactions as a potential erosion of mastery rather than necessarily a loss of purpose. Developers may still find their overall work meaningful while missing opportunities to exercise and develop their technical skills.

Solving software problems (such as CTFs, software development and design) used to involve almost a minimum level of experience among software professionals, but with the rise of AI many non-professionals now are able to create relatively high level software solutions. Additionally, the productivity benefits are noticed by professionals, who have now started to use it to write code in their workplaces (many of which have been forced to). This puts 2 strains on the Mastery force, the first is that people new to the field have skill erosion to AI completing difficult tasks, and the second is that professionals no longer need to think hard to solve these technical challenges.

Research supports distinguishing productivity from motivation. Across four online experiments, Wu et al. (2025) found improved performance with generative AI, but reduced intrinsic motivation when participants subsequently worked independently. These short task sequences do not establish long-term effects on developers, or prove that reduced mastery caused the motivational changes. Nevertheless, they challenge the assumption that better output necessarily produces a better experience of work.

In creating binary ninja however, I would argue that AI allows for newer different software challenges that previously were not possible. For example, a previous agentic studio assignment was set around creating a fake course for some fake degree (Ky787, n.d.). I took this opportunity to use AI to investigate an idea I had about generic assembly de-obfuscators where AI was used to compile hundreds of binaries, run various obfuscators, and then de-obfuscators. Finally it would run analytics through those binaries and produce detailed reports. This project used multiple toolchains, obfuscators, de-obfuscators and a complex tool suite that would be impossible for a human to do in a reasonable amount of time. For this project I had the agents prepare various examples where I would then validate assembly and other claims the agents were making. This unique dataset then laid the foundations for binary ninja (Project records, 2026a, 2026b). This scenario shows that someone specialised in a field working with AI, can start to perform new experiments and solve challenges that would without AI, be almost impossible. It also shows that someone with no experience using AI would not be able to design, guide and validate these experiments. Overall, this is a prime example of how AI can be used to enhance "Mastery" motivation.

However, feeling responsible for a sophisticated result is not proof of learning. Shen and Tamkin’s (2026) randomised coding study found lower immediate comprehension scores with AI assistance. Their exploratory analysis also associated explanation-seeking and conceptual engagement with stronger learning outcomes than extensive delegation. It did not establish that those interaction styles caused the difference. This highlights a big problem with AI in general, which is the gradual attrition of problem solving abilities.

Binary Ninja therefore illustrates both the risks and opportunities of AI-assisted development. Although AI completed much of the repetitive programming and large-scale binary analysis, the project still required considerable technical judgement, including designing experiments, validating results and deciding how to translate the research into an enjoyable game. In my experience, mastery does not necessarily require writing every line of code. It requires retaining responsibility for difficult decisions and continuing to develop the knowledge needed to make them. AI can undermine mastery through excessive delegation, but it can also enable people to tackle challenges previously beyond their reach.
